# Play store listing copy

**This document is the only copy of the listing text.** The Console is where it is pasted, not where
it lives — a value that exists only in a web form cannot be reviewed, translated as a set, or
recovered when someone edits it by accident.

Three scripts read this file, so its headings are load-bearing:

| Script | Reads |
| --- | --- |
| `scripts/play-whatsnew.py` | the `## Release notes` section, rendering every locale's note in the one format the Console's textarea accepts |
| `scripts/notes-gate.py` | the newest `### x.y.z` heading, refusing a merge where it does not match `versionName` |
| `scripts/play-metadata.py` | the whole thing, generating the `fastlane/metadata/` tree the publish workflow uploads |

> **The gate exists because of a real failure.** A release shipped to production carrying the
> *previous* version's release notes: `versionName` had been bumped and the notes had not, nothing
> compared the two, and Play does not allow release notes to be edited on a live release. The
> correction had to wait for the next upload. Renaming a heading is cheaper.

### Full description — 2577/4000

**No health or safety claims**, here or on any page the listing links to — Play's enforcement reads a
linked page as part of the listing. Say what the app cannot do instead.

The shape the apps built from this template converged on, with headings in capitals because Play
renders no markup. The block below is what `play-metadata.py` uploads, so the outline stays out of it:

1. One or two sentences: the problem, and what the app does about it.
2. `HOW IT WORKS` — what happens, in the order the user meets it.
3. `FREE, AND YOUR PHOTOS STAY ON YOUR OWN PHONES` — no ads, no account, no server; what it stores.
   No claim about the internet permission: from phase G it is declared for the Mounted camera's local
   link (ADR-0009), so the true claim is about servers, not permissions.
4. `WHAT IT CANNOT DO` — the honest limits, and what a phone or the platform can stop.
5. `COMING: A MOUNTED CAMERA` — while phase G is announced but not shipped (`phase-G.md` §1): what it
   will do and need, said as coming. The release that ships it turns this into a `HOW IT WORKS`
   bullet and drops *today* from `PERMISSIONS`, with the privacy policy's *coming*.
6. `PERMISSIONS` — one line each, in the user's words.
7. A last line pointing at the source: `github.com/maks-sredniawa/darts-scorer`.

```
Darts Scorer reads the score of a visit from a photo of the board. Take a photo after your throws, and the app finds each dart, marks it on the board and adds up the visit, offline, on your phone.

HOW IT WORKS
• After a visit, take a photo of the board with the darts still in it, or choose one already on your phone.
• The app finds the board and the darts, marks each dart on the photo and shows what it scored: single, double, treble, bull or a miss.
• A dart it is not sure of is marked Check. Drag it to where it really is, or set it on the keypad.
• Add a dart it missed, or remove one it should not have found. If it finds no board at all, enter the score yourself.
• Next Visit opens the camera for your next three darts. Close the camera without a photo, and the last result stays.

FREE, AND YOUR PHOTOS STAY ON YOUR OWN PHONES
No ads, no account, no server. Your photos are read on your phone and never uploaded to a server or sent to anyone. The app keeps no history of your visits, only its settings, such as the theme and the language.

WHAT IT CANNOT DO
• It is new, and it still gets darts wrong, most often on boards with printed decorations around the numbers, and on a dart hidden behind another. Check each dart before you trust the total.
• It reads a regulation steel-tip board, not a soft-tip or electronic one.
• It scores one visit at a time. It does not keep a game score, such as 501.
• It reads one photo at a time. Combining photos taken from several sides comes later.

COMING: A MOUNTED CAMERA
A later version will let a spare Android phone, mounted beside the board and running this app, take the photo for you. Tap on your phone after a visit; the mounted camera takes the photo and sends it to your phone, which scores it. The photo goes only between your two phones, encrypted, over your Wi-Fi or your phone's hotspot, never to a server. You pair the phones once by scanning a QR code or pasting its code. It will need both phones on the same network, the camera permission on the spare phone, and access to your local network. The version you install today does not do this yet.

PERMISSIONS
• The camera, asked the first time you tap Take a Photo, since Android requires it before the app can open your camera app; say no and you can still choose a photo already on your phone.
• With the mounted camera: the camera on the spare phone, and the local network, used only between your own two phones.
• In-app purchases: an optional tip for the developer. It unlocks nothing.

The source code is public: github.com/maks-sredniawa/darts-scorer
```

## Release notes (Play's "What's new", 500 chars per locale)

**Don't paste these by hand.** The Console takes all languages in one textarea, tagged by locale,
and `scripts/play-whatsnew.py` renders exactly that from the blocks below:

```bash
python3 scripts/play-whatsnew.py                 # newest version here, to stdout
python3 scripts/play-whatsnew.py --version 0.2.1 # a named one
```

It reformats and never retypes, so this document stays the only copy. It exits non-zero if a note is
over 500 characters, or if a language the **app** ships has no note at all — both of which Play
accepts in silence and a user then reads.

The label above each block is **parsed**, not decorative: `**<Language>** — <n>/500:` on a
line of its own, with the fenced note under it. The count is what keeps a note from reaching Play
truncated, and `play-whatsnew.py` refuses a language the app ships and this section does not.

**Write these about what changed for the person reading them.** A schema migration is not a release
note; the feature it made possible is. A release where nothing user-visible changed still needs a
heading — rename it and say so in one line, so the decision is deliberate rather than made by
default.
